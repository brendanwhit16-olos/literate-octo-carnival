The Problem Situation: Democratic Engagement Has Become an Exhaustion Engine

Bottom Line Up Front

Citizens are not disengaging due to apathy—they are disengaging because modern election tech operates as a high-friction, transactional extraction system. I am asking for your vote to endorse this specific problem situation so we can apply Frame Innovation to saturate in the problem before rushing to build a tech fix.

Modern civic platforms treat voters as data points to harvest and attention to monetize through continuous manufactured urgency. This creates structural voter fatigue, erodes institutional trust, and weakens long-term civic agency.

Before we attempt to build software, we must first deeply investigate, map, and reframe this problem arena.

5 Dimensions of the Problem Arena

Friction 01

Transactional Extraction

Civic platforms reduce citizens to data profiles used for algorithmic segmentation and endless micro-fundraising.

Friction 02

Continuous Urgency

Constant high-anxiety messaging creates cognitive overload, driving voters toward burnout and withdrawal.

Friction 03

Signal-to-Noise Decay

High volumes of low-value, coercive political content obscure clear, actionable democratic information.

Friction 04

Institutional Atrophy

Short-term turnout tactics undermine long-term community trust and sustainable democratic participation.

Friction 05

Premature Solutions

Most tech interventions fail because they digitize existing broken workflows rather than reframing the core problem.

Our Methodology: Reframing Before Building

Applying Kees Dorst’s Frame Innovation

Instead of jumping straight into wireframes, our team will use the Frame Innovation framework to thoroughly saturate in the problem space. By examining the underlying context, stakeholder behaviors, and systemic values, we will uncover a new conceptual frame for non-extractive civic interaction.

This research-first initiation ensures that any resulting technical intervention will be genuinely differentiated, remarkable, and rooted in real-world human needs.

  • Phase 1: Initiation & Context: Establishing core problem parameters.
  • Phase 2: Problem Saturation: Field research with voters & organizers.
  • Phase 3: Theme Analysis: Uncovering hidden structural patterns.
  • Phase 4: Reframing & Prototype: Designing a non-coercive intervention.
